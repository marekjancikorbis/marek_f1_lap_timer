Purple Sector, painted as a livery

A redesign proposal for every screen of the app. Paddock screens sit on the light bodywork; the drive screens are the navy panel. Every lap wears its own stripe: its three sector results as slanted bands, from the feed card up to the full-screen HUD. These are design pages only. Nothing in the app has changed, and all drivers, times and routes shown are sample data.

Sign in

The first three screens a new driver sees. The car's navy panel introduces the brand once, then the app gets out of the way.

Paddock

Where drivers spend the time between runs: the feed of people they follow, the map of routes, and profiles. Every lap card wears its stripe, so a scroll through the feed reads like a pit wall.

Routes

Drivers build their own private routes; approved creators publish them with a leaderboard. Route shapes are always drawn at the same scale and orientation, so laps on one route line up when compared.

Drive

From the light paddock into the navy car. Once Start is pressed, every screen is navy with off-white numerals sized for a glance in a windscreen mount, and nothing needs a tap until the car has stopped.

Review

After the drive: a stage gets one result, a lap route gets its session of laps; then the post, and the lap read against the lap it raced. A finished lap gets the whole first screen; the detail is one scroll or one tab away.

Account

Settings, moderation and the one screen nobody wants to see. Same light bodywork, same row grammar, so these pages need no explanation.
